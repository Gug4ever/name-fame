The Story of the Name

David comes from the Hebrew Dawid — 'beloved': the shepherd boy who felled a giant with a sling, wrote psalms on a harp, and became the king every later king was measured against. Beloved is exactly what the name has stayed — carried by saints and explorers, philosophers and rock stars, on more shoulders in more countries than almost any name alive.

The Numerology of David

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of David add up to the number 22 — “The Master Builder”. Twenty-two is the rarest number in the chart: the master builder. He is the dreamer with calluses, the visionary who also shows up on Monday morning. This number takes a shepherd's patience and aims it straight at giants, at kingdoms, at continents, at whole planets of knowledge, and then builds what everyone else only sketched, stone by smooth stone.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Uranus, the gemstone Golden Topaz, the flower Daffodil and the colour Royal Blue.
