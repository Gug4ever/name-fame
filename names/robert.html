The Story of the Name

Robert comes from the old Germanic Hrodebert, built from hrod, meaning fame, and berht, meaning bright: a name that promises shining renown. The Normans carried it to England in 1066 and it never left the top tables again. Kings of Scotland bore it, and so did poets, scientists, photographers and actors who each made the old promise true: the name still means being brilliantly, deservedly known.

The Numerology of Robert

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of Robert add up to the number 33 — “The Master Teacher”. Thirty-three is the rarest master number of all: the master teacher. It combines the biggest heart in the chart with the discipline to actually pass its gifts on. A Thirty-Three does not simply achieve things; he shows the rest of us how it is done, in poems, in classrooms, in laboratories and around campfires. What he learns the hard way, he hands over freely, and the world keeps his lessons long after his name.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Venus, the gemstone Pearl, the flower Daisy and the colour Slate.
