The Story of the Name

José is the Spanish and Portuguese form of the Hebrew Yosef, the prayer Rachel spoke over her newborn son: may God add, may God increase. The name crossed into Greek as Ioseph and Latin as Iosephus, and when it reached Iberia it lost its final consonant and gained an accent to mark the stress. It then sailed with the caravels to the whole Hispanic and Lusophone world, where it is still the commonest male name in Brazil.

The Numerology of José

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of José add up to the number 4 — “The Steady Builder”. Fours build, and a José builds for others: the name means God will add, and Number Four is the hand that lays the next stone. He is the general who spends two years drilling an army before he dares the mountains, the muralist who covers a whole chapel one wall at a time, the chef who turns a single food truck into a kitchen for a continent. A Four's patience is not slowness; it is the refusal to leave a thing half done.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Uranus, the gemstone White Opal, the flower Daffodil and the colour Pale Blue.
