The Story of the Name

Susan is the Hebrew shoshannah, the lily, and probably older still: the Egyptians called the lotus seshen, and the word may have travelled up the Nile before it reached the Bible. The Book of Daniel gave it to a woman who told the truth when lying would have been safer, and the name has kept that habit: a suffragist refusing to pay her fine, a doctor with a lamp in her window, a tennis player who threw dignity to the winds and won.

The Numerology of Susan

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of Susan add up to the number 11 — “The Lantern”. Eleven is a master number, and it works like a lamp left in a window: it does not shout, it simply stays lit, and people find their way to it. An Eleven sees the shape of a thing before others do, whether it is a hole in the sky over Antarctica or a vote that is going to be won, and then she keeps the light on, night after night, until everyone else can see it too.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet The Moon, the gemstone Aquamarine, the flower Poppy and the colour Sea Teal.
