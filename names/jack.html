The Story of the Name

Jack began as a nickname and grew into a name of its own. Medieval England shortened John to Jankin, the little John, and by the 1300s Jankin had worn down to Jack. It was so common that a jack came to mean any man at all: jack of all trades, lumberjack, every man Jack. The French Jacques, often supposed to be its parent, is in fact a form of James.

The Numerology of Jack

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of Jack add up to the number 7 — “The Quiet Worker”. Sevens do their best work where nobody is watching. They take a problem away, turn it over in private, and come back with something finished. A Jack of this number is not the loudest man in the room, but he is very often the one who has actually thought it through, and when he does speak, the answer tends to be short and to hold.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Neptune, the gemstone Golden Topaz, the flower Rose and the colour Forest Green.
