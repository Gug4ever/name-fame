The Story of the Name

Nicholas is Greek through and through: Nikolaos joins nikē, victory, to laos, the people, and the two halves have never come apart. A fourth-century bishop of Myra gave the name its saint, and the saint gave the world its gift-bringer, for Sinterklaas and Santa Claus are both his name worn down by centuries of children. Its bearers moved the Earth around the Sun, lit cities with alternating current, and put eight trains' worth of children on the road to safety.

The Numerology of Nicholas

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of Nicholas add up to the number 9 — “The Giver”. Nines close the number cycle, and they behave like people who know it: they give things away. A Nine hands the Sun its rightful place and dies before the reviews come in, sells his patents and spends the money on the next idea, or fills a scrapbook with rescued children and puts it in the attic for fifty years. The gift is the point; the credit can look after itself.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Mars, the gemstone Amethyst, the flower Narcissus and the colour Pale Blue.
