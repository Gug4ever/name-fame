Carmen

The Story of the Name

Carmen is a mountain before it is a woman. Mount Carmel, karmel in Hebrew, means the garden or the vineyard, and the hermits who settled its slopes took Mary as the lady of the place. Spanish families christened their daughters María del Carmen for her, and the world shortened it to Carmen. The Latin carmen, song, is a separate word that happens to look the same — a coincidence the poets have never wanted to give up.

The Numerology of Carmen

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of Carmen add up to the number 9 — “The Whole Heart”. Nines give everything to the thing in front of them, and a Carmen gives it visibly. She is the least half-hearted number on the chart: she works at full stretch, she minds about other people in a practical way, and she is entirely unembarrassed about either. What looks like fire from outside is usually just attention, turned all the way up and held there for years.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Mars, the gemstone Emerald, the flower Larkspur and the colour Royal Purple.
