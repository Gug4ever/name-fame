The Story of the Name

Maria is the Latin form of the Hebrew Miryam, a name whose meaning scholars still argue over: beloved, wished-for child, rebellious. Jerome's Latin Bible fixed the spelling, and he also glossed it stilla maris, drop of the sea; a copyist's slip made that stella, star, and the star of the sea stuck. It is the everyday name of the Latin world from Lisbon to Manila, and the Marias in this book include two Nobel physicists, a soprano and a ballerina.

The Numerology of Maria

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of Maria add up to the number 6 — “The Keeper”. Sixes are the keepers of the number chart: the ones who take responsibility for a room, a child, a desert full of drawings or a truth nobody else will print, and hold it. Care comes to them as a discipline rather than a mood. A Six measures her day by what is safer, better taught or better understood because she was there, and Marias have been keeping things for a very long time.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Venus, the gemstone Pearl, the flower Aster and the colour Harbour Teal.
