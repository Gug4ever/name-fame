The Story of the Name

Barbara began as a Greek word for anyone whose speech sounded like bar-bar to an Athenian ear: barbaros, the foreigner. Rome softened it into a woman's name, and a legend about a girl locked in a tower by her father turned the stranger into a saint. Her daughters have kept a knack for arriving from outside and rearranging the room: a geneticist the field ignored for thirty years, a Brooklyn singer who took Broadway at twenty-one, and a minister who put a breathalyser in every police car.

The Numerology of Barbara

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of Barbara add up to the number 7 — “The Still Water”. Sevens are the still waters of the number chart, and a Barbara shows what the stillness is for. She watches longer than other people find comfortable: a field of maize for thirty summers, a block of stone until the shape inside it declares itself, a set of laws until their unfairness is plain enough to print. Then she moves, and the room discovers that the quiet one had already decided.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Neptune, the gemstone Lapis Lazuli, the flower Narcissus and the colour Slate.
