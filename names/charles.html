The Story of the Name

Charles comes from the Germanic karl — 'free man': not a king's name at first, but everyman's. Then one Karl became Charlemagne, Charles the Great, and the name of the free man became the name of emperors — carried since by kings of France, Spain, Sweden and Britain, and by free-thinking commoners named Darwin, Dickens and Chaplin who out-reigned them all.

The Numerology of Charles

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of Charles add up to the number 3 — “The Creator”. Threes are the storytellers of the number chart, and expression is their engine. Ideas arrive faster than they can possibly be written, sketched, invented or acted out, and yet somehow all of it lands on its feet. It is the number of wit, charm and creative abundance: the kind of mind that makes something new every single day, and makes it look easy.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Jupiter, the gemstone Emerald, the flower Chrysanthemum and the colour Rose Pink.
