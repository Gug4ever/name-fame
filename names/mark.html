The Story of the Name

Mark is the English form of Marcus, one of the handful of first names a Roman boy could be given, and most scholars trace it to Mars, the god of war whose month opens the Roman year. It came into the Christian world through the Evangelist, the author of the shortest Gospel, whose lion stands over Venice. Its bearers have kept the Roman plainness: a name of four letters, said in one breath, that has belonged to a river pilot, a tunneller and a traveller to China.

The Numerology of Mark

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of Mark add up to the number 7 — “The Patient Digger”. Sevens go under the surface and stay there until the work is done. A Mark of this number is not in a hurry to be understood: he would rather spend eighteen years under a river, or seventeen at a foreign court, than a season being admired. When he comes back up, he brings the thing itself, finished, and lets it speak for him.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Neptune, the gemstone Golden Topaz, the flower Daisy and the colour Garden Green.
