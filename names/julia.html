The Story of the Name

Julia is the feminine voice of Julius, the ancient Roman clan name that tradition traced to Iulus, son of Aeneas, and glossed as youthful, soft-haired, touched by Jove. Rome's most powerful women carried it first, and it has kept its promise of youth ever since: Julias have photographed beauty before anyone else dared, written battle hymns, built castles on California hills, fed a whole country butter, and lived in a redwood tree to save it.

The Numerology of Julia

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of Julia add up to the number 8 — “The Gentle Powerhouse”. Eights build things that last, and they build them their own way. They are the ones who found cities where others see swamp, raise castles on empty hilltops, and hold a television audience with nothing but a whisk and total confidence. An Eight's power rarely announces itself; it simply keeps working, season after season, until one day everyone is living inside what she made.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Saturn, the gemstone Aquamarine, the flower Lily of the Valley and the colour Slate.
