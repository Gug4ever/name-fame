The Story of the Name

Rose is the flower worn as a name. The Romans called it rosa, and the word travelled with the plant: through Old French rose into Norman England, where it arrived first as a Germanic name, Hrodohaidis, bright kind, and was soon quietly re-read as the flower everyone already loved. Its bearers have grown the way roses do: from a hard root, with thorns where needed, and blooming late as often as early.

The Numerology of Rose

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of Rose add up to the number 3 — “The Wonder-Maker”. Threes are the imagination of the number chart, and a Rose of this number does what the flower does: she makes something lovely out of plain ground. She has a voice and uses it, on a bus, in a courtroom, in a union hall, on an opera stage, and she says the necessary thing in the fewest words. A Three does not wait for permission. She has, as Rosa Bonheur put it, no patience with people who ask for it.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Jupiter, the gemstone Pearl, the flower Poppy and the colour Warm Gold.
