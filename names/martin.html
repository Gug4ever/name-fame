The Story of the Name

Martin comes from the Latin Martinus, a family name built on Mars, the Roman god of war, and it entered Christian Europe on the shoulders of one man: a soldier who cut his cloak in half for a beggar and became the bishop of Tours. His feast, Martinmas, fell in every calendar, and so the name fell into every language: Martín in Spain, Martino in Italy, Marcin in Poland, Maarten in the Netherlands. The Martins who followed named a continent, split a church and dreamed aloud in Washington.

The Numerology of Martin

In the Pythagorean tradition, the letters of Martin add up to the number 3 — “The Voice”. Threes are the communicators of the number chart: the people who cannot see a thing clearly without wanting to say it clearly, and who find, when they do, that a room has gone quiet to listen. A Martin of this number writes the theses, draws the map, makes the call. What he knows is not finished, for him, until it has been shared.

Its four emblems in classical tradition: the planet Jupiter, the gemstone Golden Topaz, the flower Chrysanthemum and the colour Pale Blue.
